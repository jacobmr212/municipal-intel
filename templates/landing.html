The challenge

Most municipalities don't know where their financial systems actually stand.

Compliance blind spots

State reporting requirements change annually. Without a systematic review, gaps accumulate silently until audit season arrives.

Payroll complexity grows

Special pay types, deferred comp, multi-jurisdiction taxes — legacy systems struggle as your payroll structure evolves beyond their design.

No objective baseline

When vendors pitch new systems, you have no independent assessment to compare against. Every proposal looks compelling without context.

After your assessment

What happens next is up to you

Your report is yours to use however you see fit. Here are three common paths municipalities take, each with clear benefits depending on where you are in your ERP journey.

Internal planning

Best for: Finance teams building a modernization roadmap

Use your diagnostic report to prioritize IT investments, plan process improvements, and build a business case before engaging vendors.

  • Identify quick wins vs. long-term projects
  • Budget estimates for remediation costs
  • Staff training needs assessment

Council presentation

Best for: Municipalities needing decision-maker buy-in

Share the report with your city council or board to secure budget approval and support for financial system improvements.

  • Third-party validation of concerns
  • Clear risk communication for non-technical stakeholders
  • Data-backed business case for funding

Expert connection

Best for: Municipalities ready to explore ERP solutions

Opt in to share your results with vetted ERP implementation specialists who can help you act on the findings.

  • Skip cold vendor outreach — consultants contact you
  • Specialists see your gaps before the first call
  • Optional — you control who sees your report
